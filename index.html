How this is calculated, and its limits
  1. Slider to gain. The slider type turns position into an attenuation in dB. Gain = 10^(dB ÷ 20).
  2. Voltage and power. Volts = max output voltage × gain. Power P = V² ÷ Z.
  3. Level. SPL = sensitivity (dB/mW) + 10·log10(P in mW) + content offset. A dB/V rating is converted first: dB/mW = dB/V − 10·log10(1000 ÷ Z).
  4. Safe time. NIOSH allows 85 dBA for 8 hours and halves the time every 3 dB above: T = 8 h ÷ 2^((L − 85) ÷ 3).
  • It uses spec-sheet figures. It is a model, not a measurement. Only a calibrated meter or ear coupler measures the real level.
  • The Windows, Android and Apple curves are approximations. Drivers and manufacturers change the real gain.
  • Bluetooth headphones and many wireless dongles add their own gain on top of the system slider, which this does not model.
  • Levels are unweighted while NIOSH limits are in dBA, so the result is conservative for bass-heavy music.
  • Ear coupling shifts the level by a few dB. Limits are population averages, not medical advice. If your ears ring or sound muffled after listening, turn it down.
